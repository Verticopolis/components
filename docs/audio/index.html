Audio

Snapshot of the source on 2026-10-15, moved here 2026-11-20. Every number here (gains, rates, frequencies, timings, budgets) is copied by hand from the source and does not refresh. The files named in each section hold the live values.

How the package makes noise: the modules in src/audio/ (@verticopolis/components/audio/*), the bus graph, the lifecycle, the cue vocabulary and the ambient layer. Below, “the audio engine” means ToneAudioEngine and “the facade” means AudioEngine in Audio.ts.

What fires a cue, what the player’s settings are, and which sounds are planned next belong to the game that hosts the audio: the web game’s side is SOUNDS.md in Verticopolis/web. Issue numbers (#N) on this page are Verticopolis/web’s, where it lived until 2026-11-20.

The goal

Section titled “The goal”

The 1994 original’s soundscape was dynamic in a specific way. It was a building you could hear: sounds arrived at their own rates, layered without coordinating, and changed with the hour and with where you were looking.

  • Probability. An elevator arrival sometimes made a sound and sometimes stayed quiet. That is most of why it never became tiring; a cue that fires on every instance of a frequent event becomes noise within a minute.
  • Stacking. Typing, registers, whirs, and the crowd ran at once at independent rates, and the density of the pile was the information.
  • Time of day. Birds at sunrise, an owl at night. They tell you the hour while you are looking at something else, and give the tower a park-like quality a purely mechanical building would lack.

The crowd layer already has this machinery. When weighing a new sound, ask whether it thickens that soundscape or merely announces an event.

The short version

Section titled “The short version”
  • Sound is Tone.js, synthesized live. Excalibur’s audio system is not used at all: Excalibur draws the tower and nothing more. No ex.Sound, no audio Loader, no per-cue asset files.
  • Almost nothing is sampled. The only recordings are audio/voice-talk.mp3 and audio/voice-laughs.mp3 (audioAssets.ts), gibberish talk and laughter the owner recorded, which the crowd layer chops into murmur. The host serves them under its base URL. Everything else is generated at run time.
  • The music and action cues are transcriptions of the owner’s own recordings (hums, chest hits, taps, a bloop, a ping). New cues should speak in that voice rather than introduce a fresh instrument.
  • Every sound is original to Verticopolis, UI effects included: synthesized in code, rendered from the project’s code, or recorded by the owner. No third-party sample or sound pack ships, free or paid.
  • The audio layer only plays. The host tells it what to play (sfx(name)), where the camera is (update(focus)), the program and the player’s levels. It never decides a game rule.

ViewFocus is what the scene reports about its camera (src/render/excalibur/towerInputCamera.ts): the floor at the middle of the view, the zoom, the dominant kind, night, the hour, the weather and how full the rooms in view are. A host hands the latest one to update about six times a second.

The bus graph

Section titled “The bus graph”
  • musicBus carries the composed tracks and percussion. The Music slider. Its output goes through the shared reverb.
  • ambBus and ambDryBus carry the crowd layer, room tone, and rain. The Ambience slider. Two nodes because rain stays dry while the rest shares the reverb.
  • sfxBus carries the action jingles. The Effects slider.
  • master is downstream of all of them (directly, or through the shared reverb) at gain 0.35; mute ramps it to zero.

A bedFilter lowpass on the ambient path opens as the camera zooms in, so distance muffles the building’s life. Music bypasses it.

The melodic voices reach musicBus through a short chain: a level gain, a warm 2400 Hz lowpass, then a 90 Hz sub highpass. Percussion skips that chain and connects to musicBus itself, so the tap stays bright and the thump keeps its body; it still sits behind the Music slider, the shared reverb, and mute.

Three sliders plus mute is the whole player-facing surface. Levels live in Prefs, are clamped in the facade before reaching an AudioParam, and are read back from the facade so prefs never store junk.

Lifecycle

Section titled “Lifecycle”

Browsers refuse to make noise before a gesture, and the Tone.js chunk is large (about 230 kB minified), so both are handled together. The host builds the facade at boot with nothing heavy loaded. The first real gesture calls start(), which feature-detects AudioContext (so tests and unsupported environments never fetch the chunk), then dynamically imports ToneAudioEngine. Once the audio engine exists the facade replays what it was holding: mute, volumes, program, last focus, and the cue the unlocking gesture fired (firstCueLatch.ts).

stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> Inert: facade built at boot
    Inert --> Loading: start() on a gesture
    Loading --> Inert: no AudioContext
    Loading --> Running: chunk loaded, state replayed
    Running --> Away: tab hidden or focus lost
    Away --> Running: player back
    Running --> [*]: dispose()
    Loading --> [*]: dispose() (generation guard)

While the chunk loads, a second start() returns early. A generation counter guards the load window, so a dispose() mid-flight cannot resurrect a torn-down audio engine.

setProgram("splash" | "game") picks the track; the splash theme crossfades to the in-game bed on entry.

Leaving the page silences it. awayWatch.ts reports the player away while the tab is hidden or the page has lost focus, and setAway fades the master out, skips the music part’s notes and any cues, quiets the crowd, and suspends the context once the fade has played, so the music holds its place (ContextSleep in toneContext.ts). Coming back resumes the context at the player’s own mute. This is kept apart from the player’s mute. It fixed static heard in a background tab: a backgrounded or unfocused page gets a throttled, lower-priority main thread, so the notes it builds only 0.1 s ahead (Tone’s look-ahead) arrived late and crackled.

An installed game has to play every sound offline. AUDIO_RUNTIME_FILES and the chunk name in audioAssets.ts are what a host’s precache check reads; audioAssets.test.ts fails when the source names an audio/... file the list lacks, and audioFetches.test.ts fails when the audio engine loads one.

The cue vocabulary

Section titled “The cue vocabulary”

SfxName is the complete list of one-shot cues. All play through sfxBus.

Cue Sound

error A slow, sighing double bloop
promote The splash theme’s peak turn on the ping bell

Where the web game fires each one is in its SOUNDS.md.

The ambient layer

Section titled “The ambient layer”

The cues are the small part. The soundscape lives in toneCrowd.ts with its data in toneCrowdData.ts, and it is closer to the original’s behavior than the cue table suggests: the stacking, rate-driven, occupancy-scaled machinery is already built. What is missing is mostly content and time-of-day gating.

A scene resolves from camera focus and the dominant facility kind: lobby, office, condo, hotel, restaurant, fastFood, shop, cinema, partyHall, metro, outside. Overview, quiet, and service areas resolve to nothing and get music and room tone only. Each scene carries a murmur spec, a list of elements (one-shot details on independent timers), a gate, optionally a composed program, and optionally a crowdFloor for the city’s own spaces, which never fall fully silent (indoor rooms have none, so empty means silent).

Everything is scheduled on timers seeded by the pseudo hash, so the layer varies without Math.random and without a fixed loop becoming audible. outside also swells a low street hum (two oscillators under an LFO) while it is live.

How firing works

Section titled “How firing works”

There is no per-event probability roll. Each element runs a loop that sleeps a drawn gap and fires:

  • rateMin/rateVar describe a gap. Bigger means rarer.
  • Low activity stretches the gap, up to 5x. The clamp floors at 0.2, so a near-empty office types at a fifth of its busy rate instead of falling silent. This is what produces “quiet but alive.”
  • Zero activity idles the loop. When the gate reads 0 (an office at 3 a.m.) or the crowd is below 5% (EMPTY_CROWD), activity is exactly zero: the loop fires nothing and only re-checks every couple of seconds. The 0.2 floor applies only while activity is above zero. metro and outside set a crowdFloor, so their crowd never drops below 5%.

So a sound’s “probability” is really its average firings per minute, which is what the tables give, quoted at full activity. Multiply by activity for any other moment, down to the 0.2 floor. Camera position picks the scene. Zooming out past the overview threshold (OVERVIEW_ZOOM 0.55, left again at 0.63) switches to the overview scene, which has no crowd layer, so nothing fires. Within a scene, zoom only scales the layer’s level and opens bedFilter.

Element sounds

Section titled “Element sounds”

ping and thud are pitched tones; burst is a noise hit whose frequency column is a filter cutoff. Attack defaults to 0.004 s (a tick); longer is a swish. “Companion” is the pair field: a second hit at that ratio, delay, and relative gain. The per-minute figures count firings: every hit of a cluster counts (a cluster of N hits has N - 1 short gaps, then the pause), and a companion note does not, so the bird’s ~11 firings a minute are ~22 chirps.

lobby, cinema, partyHall, and metro have no elements in this table. The last three run a composed program from toneCrowdPrograms.ts instead, while the venue is live:

  • Party hall: the band plays a 124 BPM remix of the splash hook, and the crowd layer adds the owner’s laughs (every 18 to 40 s) and voice-bent whoops (every 22 to 46 s).
  • Cinema: a sparse plucked score under muffled dialogue, with a riser into a boom every 8 to 14 s.
  • Metro: a scripted train arrival and departure about every 55 s (jittered by up to 20 percent), over a low rumble.

Under every scene sits the room-tone bed (pink noise behind a steep lowpass, set per scene in SCENES), and rain joins on the dry path when the weather calls for it.

Look at the bird row twice. Outside, it chirps whenever you are looking, at any hour, because its gate is always: about eleven times a minute with a full street, and still about four a minute at the street’s 0.35 crowd floor.

Murmur

Section titled “Murmur”

Talker count scales with live occupancy, so the count listed is the ceiling (at least one talker while the room is live). Each talker loops: one phrase of 0.9 to 1.8 seconds (about 1.35 s on average), then a pause drawn from pauseMin + draw × pauseVar. “Per talker” counts phrases a minute over that whole cycle, so a full lobby is six talkers each starting a phrase about every 2.4 seconds.

A pitch offset means every talker shares one fixed shift (the office phone call, the condo TV, the cinema dialogue). Blank means each talker draws its own from the down-only range.

Voices are the special case: grains chopped from the two seed mp3s by toneCrowdVoices.ts, which screens candidate windows by zero-crossing rate and RMS so squeals and shouts are never sampled. Everything else is synthesized.

Gate curves

Section titled “Gate curves”

hourActivity(gate, hour) is the entire time-of-day model today:

  • workday is 0 before 8, ramps linearly to 1 by 9, holds until 17, ramps back to 0 by 19, and stays 0 overnight.
  • condoDay is 0 before 7, steps to 1 at 7, steps down to 0.2 at 9, ramps back to 1 from 15 to 17, holds until 21, ramps to 0 by 23, and stays 0 overnight.
  • always and attendance are 1 at every hour.
Hour 0 7 8 9 15 16 17 18 19 21 22 23
workday 0 0 0 1 1 1 1 0.5 0 0 0 0
condoDay 0 1 1 0.2 0.2 0.6 1 1 1 1 0.5 0

attendance is 1 at every hour by design: the live crowd factor is its gate. always ignores the hour; its activity still follows the crowd factor.

The missing row is the point: no curve peaks at dawn and none peaks at night. See the dawn birds idea below.

Recordings instead of tones

Section titled “Recordings instead of tones”

Replacing a synthesized element with a recording is an open idea. It is a new sound, so it needs an owner audition. If it is picked up, these constraints already apply:

  • The recording is the project’s own. Owner-recorded or rendered from the project’s code; no third-party sample or pack, CC0 included. It gets an entry in the license record of the repository that ships the file.
  • It is listed for the offline precache. It gets a named constant in audioAssets.ts holding its full path (for example "audio/bird-chirp.mp3"), added to AUDIO_RUNTIME_FILES. Code loads it as `${baseUrl}${CONSTANT}`, the way the voice seeds load.
  • It is an mp3. A host precaches mp3 and no other audio type.
  • Match the seeds’ encoding unless a clip proves it needs more: 22050 Hz, mono, 32 kbps. The talk seed is 18 s in 72 kB and the laugh seed 2.6 s in 11 kB. Decoded audio is resampled to the context’s rate and stored as 32-bit floats, so memory depends on duration and channel count, never on bitrate.
  • The plumbing is private today. CrowdVoices (toneCrowdVoices.ts) plays its grains through a private emit, and the crowd layer fires elements through synths; a clip player, its destination node and its gain scaling would be new design work.

House rules

Section titled “House rules”

Breaking one tends to produce a bug that shows up only on someone else’s hardware or in a replay.

  • Never draw from the game’s seeded random numbers. Sound is presentation, and a game’s seeded streams are part of its save determinism. Vary with the pseudo hash seeded on things that already move, never with Math.random.
  • No SfxName cue ramps below 160 Hz. The owner tested the action cues on phone and laptop speakers and could not hear lower floors (the audibility rule in toneVoices.ts). Deep cues add quiet octave and twelfth partials so small speakers reconstruct the pitch. The rule covers the cues on sfxBus; the ambient elements are outside it (several thuds sit between 64 and 115 Hz).
  • Guard retriggers. ERROR_HOLDOFF is the model: a 0.65 s window keyed to the voice set by WeakMap, with an explicit check for a rewound Tone clock.
  • Keep the pure modules pure. toneScenes.ts and toneTracks.ts carry no Tone.js import and no live state, which lets tests assert note data directly.
  • Celebrations ring the ping bell. promote and notify play on the ping bell (strikeBell); the bloops are for build, sell, click, and error.
  • Audio only reads. It reads the focus it is handed and never writes back to the game.

Adding a cue

Section titled “Adding a cue”

A new sound needs the owner’s OK before it merges (CLAUDE.md, design sign-off). Then:

  1. Add the name to the SfxName union in toneScenes.ts.
  2. Add its recipe as a case in playSfx, reusing existing voices where you can. A new instrument means a new SfxVoices field and its construction in createSfxVoices. Nothing else is needed for teardown: ToneAudioEngine.dispose() walks every SfxVoices value.
  3. Tests: toneVoices.test.ts for the recipe, toneAudioEngineGraph.test.ts for routing, toneAudioEngine.test.ts for the inert-without-audio contract.
  4. Add it to the gallery’s sound board (gallery/sounds.ts) so it can be heard and reviewed.
  5. Add a change note (.changes/README.md).

The game then fires it with sfx("name"). The facade no-ops before the audio engine loads, which is correct.

Hearing the current sounds

Section titled “Hearing the current sounds”

The gallery’s sound board (npm run gallery, gallery/sounds.ts) plays every action cue, both music tracks and the ambience of each part of a tower. There is no checked-in way to render the sounds to a file yet.

An earlier draft of this doc carried a local render script. It was dropped because nothing tested it and it re-implemented the crowd layer’s synths, so it could drift from the game unnoticed. The lessons from it are worth carrying into a renderer:

  • Render through the master gain (0.35) or build, sell, and error clip, which makes a recipe sound hotter than it is.
  • Normalize per group (cues, ambience), never per item. At the master, office typing sits around 50 dB below the error cue. That is right in game and inaudible in a sample, and per-item normalizing would flatten the balance you are listening for.
  • Trim silence per sound before joining, or every later timestamp drifts.
  • burst elements are pink noise, so their level varies a few dB run to run; compare a ping or thud render to tell whether a recipe changed.
  • Lay out the sample for listening. A horn every 20 s makes an unusable sample, so space hits for timbre and judge rates from the tables above.
