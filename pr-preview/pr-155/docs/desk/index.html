The desk

On a computer, and on a touchscreen with room for it, the game runs as a 1994 desktop: each part of the game is its own window on a teal desk, with a taskbar along the bottom. A smaller touchscreen gets the pocket desk, where the tower fills the screen (see The pocket desk). This package holds the desk’s windows, its window menu, its taskbar, its geometry and its stylesheets. A game decides when the desk comes up and which desk it gets, which of its own elements become windows, and what the taskbar’s Start menu holds and does. The component gallery shows the desk live and where its windows open.

What the package gives a game

Section titled “What the package gives a game”
Import What it is
windows/desk/deskWindow DeskWindow, one window on the desk: minimize, maximize and restore, the size grip, the focus, and a move by the title bar. buildFrame draws a frame for content that has no window of its own.
windows/desk/deskTaskbar DeskTaskbar, the taskbar: the Start button and its menu, a button per open window, and the tray (the Control Panel’s monitor, the speaker and the clock). shownItems and trayClock are the pure parts it draws from.
windows/desk/deskPocket deskMode (which desk a screen gets), pocketBody (the grid inside the pocket tower window), the body classes DESK_TOUCH_CLASS and DESK_POCKET_CLASS, POCKET_BODY_CLASS, and the media queries TOUCH_MQ and TOUCH_DESK_MQ.
windows/desk/deskControlPanel ControlPanel, the Control Panel’s window: the large icons the game picks from the four the package draws (ControlIcon), their names and tooltips, an object count, and a ✕. Each press goes to the game; setLabel renames an icon, such as “Sounds: Off”.
windows/desk/deskWindowMenu WindowMenu, the menu a right-click on a title bar or a taskbar button opens (Restore, Minimize, Maximize, Close), with windowMenuState and runWindowCommand.
windows/desk/deskHelp buildHelpWindow, the Help window: the Player’s Manual in a page frame under its own menu bar (a MenuBarModel the game gives, with menus.render for fresh check marks; Alt stays with the game’s bar; dispose takes its page listeners off) and Contents, Back, Forward and Open in Browser, with setNav turning Back and Forward on and off. The game decides what the menus and buttons do and which page the frame shows.
windows/desk/deskGeometry Pure functions over plain numbers: defaultLayout (where each window opens the first time), fitRect (a saved place kept on a smaller screen), resizedTo (a resize held to its limits) and parseLayout (a saved layout read back safely), with DESK_WINDOW_IDS, the size limits and the layout constants.
windows/movableWindow MovableWindow, the drag by the title bar that every movable window uses, the desk’s included.
styles/game/desk.css The look: every desk window, the taskbar, the Start menu, the tray, the window menu and the Control Panel’s icons. Part of styles.css.
styles/game/desk-pocket.css The desk on a touchscreen: the touch chrome and the pocket desk. Part of styles.css.
windows/desk/deskTitle DeskTitle, the title screen on the desk: the window’s title bar and the taskbar around the page’s splash, on a computer or (show(true)) the pocket desk. Given controlPanel, it also holds the Control Panel, opened by openControl() or the tray’s monitor over the title window, so the Start menu can read the same over the title screen as over a tower.
styles/game/desk-title.css The title screen on the desk: the splash as a window over the teal desk, and where its Control Panel opens. Its buttons are the scheme’s plain push buttons, except the main one (Continue, or New Tower with no tower to continue), which keeps the default ring and bold text and wears the scheme’s accent: amber in Classic, violet in Skyline (schemes/skyline.css), yellow in High Contrast (schemes/high-contrast.css). Part of styles.css.
styles/game/desk-title-pocket.css The title screen on the pocket desk: the same window fitted to a phone, upright and sideways. Part of styles.css.

The taskbar, its Start menu and the tray are drawn here (DeskTaskbar); the game names the menu’s items and decides what each press does. The Control Panel’s window is drawn here too (ControlPanel); the game names its icons and decides what each one opens.

Putting a window on the desk

Section titled “Putting a window on the desk”

A game hands DeskWindow a DeskWindowSpec for each window:

  • id: the window’s name, which the window menu and the saved layout use.
  • el: the window. Either a frame from buildFrame(doc, id, title, close, icon) (the game moves its content into the frame’s body), or a window the game already draws with its own .win-title and ✕. The desk adds the title bar’s icon, then minimize, and maximize where it is allowed, in front of the window’s own ✕.
  • label: the name on its taskbar button and in its buttons’ labels.
  • icon: the URL of the title bar’s icon, for a window that draws its own title bar; ./favicon.png beside the page when absent. A buildFrame frame already has its icon (the icon it was given), and DeskWindow leaves it alone.
  • layer: app for the tower, which stays under the small windows even when maximized, or tool for the small windows, which stack among themselves.
  • limits: the smallest size for a window that resizes (TOWER_LIMITS, TOOLS_LIMITS, HELP_LIMITS), or null for a window of one size. heightOnly keeps the width (Tools).
  • canMax: whether it maximizes (the tower and Help do in the game).
  • isOpen, open, close: whether the game has it open, and how the game opens and closes it. A window without close never closes on the desk (Info and Tools, so nothing the player plays with can be lost).
  • changed: heard after a move, a resize, a minimize, a restore, a maximize and an un-maximize, for the taskbar, the saved layout and the analytics.
  • pressed: heard on any press in the window, so the game can bring it to the front.

DeskWindow adds three classes the stylesheet reads: desk-minimized, desk-maximized and desk-active (the window with the focus, whose title bar is navy; every other title bar is gray). The game sets desk on <body> while the desk is up; the window rules are keyed on it, so the same windows keep the game’s ordinary layout on a phone or the title screen. dispose takes everything the desk added off again.

A window’s life

Section titled “A window’s life”
stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> Normal: opened
  Normal --> Minimized: minimize button, window menu,<br/>or its taskbar button while in front
  Minimized --> Normal: its taskbar button, the game's<br/>Window menu, or Restore
  Normal --> Maximized: maximize button, double-click on<br/>the title bar, or Maximize
  Maximized --> Normal: restore button, double-click,<br/>or Restore
  Maximized --> Minimized: minimize
  Normal --> Closed: its ✕ or Close (when the game can close it)
  Maximized --> Closed: its ✕ or Close
  Closed --> Normal: the game opens it again
  • A minimized window is hidden but still laid out, so the tower’s canvas keeps its size while it waits on the taskbar.
  • A maximized window fills the desk and remembers the place it had, which a restore puts back. It has no size grip and does not move.
  • Windows stack in the order they were pressed, and only the last one pressed has a lit title bar.

Resizing

Section titled “Resizing”

A window with limits gets a size grip at its bottom right corner. Nothing resizes while the pointer moves: a dotted outline shows the size, and the window takes it once, on release, so the tower’s canvas resizes once per resize.

sequenceDiagram
  participant P as Pointer
  participant G as Size grip
  participant D as DeskWindow
  participant Geo as resizedTo
  P->>G: press
  G->>D: start: the window's rect, the pointer's spot
  D->>D: draw the dotted outline
  loop each move
    P->>G: move
    G->>Geo: start rect, the move, the limits, the desk's size
    Geo-->>D: the size asked for, within the limits and the desk
    D->>D: move the outline
  end
  P->>G: release
  D->>D: take the outline away, take the size
  D-->>D: changed("resize")

The window menu

Section titled “The window menu”

A right-click on a window’s title bar or taskbar button, or Shift+F10 or the context menu key on a focused taskbar button, opens WindowMenu for that window: Restore, Minimize, Maximize and Close. windowMenuState grays each one that does not apply: Restore while the window is in front at its own size, Minimize while it is minimized, Maximize for a window that cannot maximize or already has, Close for a window the game never closes. Up and Down move through the items; Escape or a press outside closes the menu. runWindowCommand runs the pick, bringing the window to the front first for Restore and Maximize, as 1995 did.

The taskbar

Section titled “The taskbar”

new DeskTaskbar(doc, options) draws the taskbar; the game puts its root under the desk, calls start() once it is on the page and stop() when the desk goes away. render(entries) shows one button per open window, each a TaskEntry (id, label, active, minimized); the window in front and not minimized is pressed in. setSound(on) shows the sound’s state on the speaker.

Option What it does
items The Start menu, in order: each a StartItem (id, label), a null for a separator
shown Whether an item shows, asked each time the menu opens; a separator with nothing after it goes too
start Heard with an item’s id when it is picked
task Heard with a window’s id when its button is pressed
controlPanel Opens the Control Panel from the tray’s monitor; without it the tray has no monitor
sound Turns the sound on or off from the tray’s speaker; without it the tray has no speaker
windowMenu The WindowMenu handlers (state, run); without them a right-click on a button does nothing. openWindowMenu(id, x, y) opens it from a title bar
icon, className, now The button icon (DESK_ICON when absent), a class beside desk-taskbar, and the clock’s time (the computer’s when absent)
opened, tray Heard when the menu opens and when a tray button is pressed, for the analytics

The menu opens upward with its first item focused. Up and Down move through it, Escape closes it and puts the focus back on its button, and a press anywhere else closes it. The clock reads like the 1995 tray (4:34 PM) and wakes once a minute.

Where windows open

Section titled “Where windows open”

defaultLayout(deskW, deskH) places every window the first time the desk comes up:

Window Where it opens
Tools Top left, TOOLS_WIDTH (184 px) wide, as tall as the desk up to 600 px
Info A strip across the top, between Tools and the right-hand column
Tower Under Info, between Tools and the column, down to the foot of the desk; at least 320 by 240
Map The top of the right-hand column, SIDE_COLUMN (300 px) wide
Bulletin The foot of the column; on a desk too short for it under the Map, over the tower’s sky at the left
Tower Info Under Info, left of the column
Control Panel The middle of the desk

Windows of one size take their own size, so only their corner counts. A saved place goes through fitRect when the desk opens: a window bigger than the desk shrinks to fit (never below its limits), and its title bar stays on the screen so it can always be dragged back. parseLayout reads a saved layout and drops anything that is not a usable rectangle, so a damaged store opens that window’s default place.

The pocket desk

Section titled “The pocket desk”

deskMode(screen) decides which desk a screen gets. The game reads the screen (TOUCH_MQ, TOUCH_DESK_MQ and its own computer and width checks) and sets the body class that goes with the answer:

The screen deskMode Body classes
A computer with room for the desk desk desk
A touchscreen at least 1024 by 600 (a sideways tablet, a 12.9 inch iPad held upright) desk desk, desk-touch
A smaller touchscreen (a phone held either way, a smaller upright tablet) pocket desk, desk-touch, desk-pocket
Anything else null none: the game’s own layout

A tester’s switch overrides the screen: off gives no desk, pocket the pocket desk anywhere, and any the full desk on any screen at least tablet-wide.

On a touchscreen (body.desk-touch) the taskbar and the menu rows are 44 px tall, each title-bar button has a 44 px touch area, and the Start menu opens just above the taller taskbar. When a page opts into a phone’s cutout area (viewport-fit=cover in its viewport meta), the desk area keeps in from the safe-area insets on every side and the taskbar pads its sides and foot, so no window or tool sits under a notch. On the pocket desk (body.desk-pocket) the tower window fills the desk and never moves; it has no Minimize or Maximize button. The game builds pocketBody(doc) inside it and moves Info, Tools and the tower’s stage into that grid; the stylesheet stacks them on an upright phone and puts Tools at the left on a sideways phone or an upright tablet. On an upright phone the pinned Inspect and Bulldoze show their icons alone, and Info’s traffic chip shows its meter and word without the “Traffic” label, so the five rating stars and “Backed up” fit a 360 px phone. The Map floats over the tower at its phone size; on a sideways phone, where the tower’s view is short, it may take that view’s whole height, and the map keeps its shape in the middle of the card. The Bulletin, Tower Info and the Control Panel open across the top of the desk at the height of what they hold, with the tower showing below; the Bulletin stops at a third of the desk and its log scrolls. A drag by the title bar slides any of them, or the Map, to another spot inside the desk. The taskbar switches between them. On an upright phone the seven Tools tabs share the strip’s width, and the tools row scrolls sideways with no fade, so its last tool shows whole. The Start button shows its icon alone and each window’s button its word alone. The gallery’s Pocket desk page shows a sample one live, in a phone-shaped frame.

The class contract

Section titled “The class contract”

desk.css and desk-pocket.css style the desk’s pieces. Their class names are the contract:

Class What it is
.desk-area The desk the windows sit on, above the taskbar
.desk-taskbar The 28 px bar along the bottom (DeskTaskbar draws it and everything in it)
.desk-start, .desk-start-menu, .desk-start-stripe, .desk-start-list The Start button and the menu it opens upward, with its stripe down the side and its .mb-item rows
.desk-tasks, .desk-task (.on while in front) One button per open window
.desk-tray, .desk-tray-display, .desk-tray-sound (.muted), .desk-tray-clock The tray: the Control Panel’s monitor, the speaker and the clock
.desk-win-menu The window menu (WindowMenu draws it)
.desk-control-icons, .desk-control-icon, .desk-control-status The Control Panel’s icons and its status line
.desk-outline, .desk-grip The resize outline and the size grip (DeskWindow draws them)
body.desk-touch, body.desk-pocket The touch chrome and the pocket desk (DESK_TOUCH_CLASS, DESK_POCKET_CLASS)
.desk-pocket-body The grid inside the pocket tower window (pocketBody draws it)

The title screen on the desk (desk-title.css) is keyed on body.desk-title: the splash sits in a window with a .desk-title-bar over the teal desk, with the taskbar below it. DeskTitle.show(true) puts it on the pocket desk instead and adds body.desk-title-pocket (DESK_TITLE_POCKET_CLASS, desk-title-pocket.css): the window keeps its menu bar and fills the screen above the 44 px taskbar, under the browser’s own bars. On an upright phone the poster fills its top and the buttons stack in the gray bar below (two by two on a short screen); held sideways, the buttons stand in a gray panel beside the poster. The poster takes the height the buttons leave and the premise line is hidden. On a screen too short even for that the window scrolls, so every button and the credit line can be reached. The toast rail (#toast-wrap) moves to the foot of the poster, so a notice shown with the title screen up covers neither the logo nor the buttons; the menu bar and the taskbar, with the menus they open, draw over it. A second show switches between the two, as turning a tablet can.

Cost

Section titled “Cost”

Nothing on the desk runs per frame. A move writes two styles per pointer move, a resize draws an outline and sets the size once on release, the window menu is drawn once and re-rendered (only the grayed items change) each time it opens, the taskbar re-renders when a window opens, closes, minimizes or takes the focus and once a minute for its clock, and the geometry is plain arithmetic.
